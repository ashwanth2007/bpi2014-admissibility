\subsection{Multi-objective assignment}
\label{sec:assignment-results}

The protocol runs five operational baselines, standard NSGA-II, NSGA-III as a many-objective
control, the proposed PAA-NSGA-II, its three single-component ablations, and two post-hoc
exploratory combinations. Five arrival batches, 30 seeded repeats each, 150 generations,
population 60. All \num{1225} runs completed with \textbf{zero capacity violations}, so the repair
operator satisfies constraint C2 throughout.

\paragraph{The proposed operators improve on their own base algorithm.}
Against standard NSGA-II over 150 paired runs, PAA-NSGA-II is better on four of five metrics
by paired Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm-Bonferroni correction: hypervolume
($+\num{0.00666}$, $p = \num{1.5e-19}$), first-time-right ($+\num{0.01367}$,
$p = \num{1.3e-5}$), breach ($-\num{0.01322}$, $p = \num{1.5e-4}$) and expertise match
($+\num{0.04898}$, $p = \num{9.9e-7}$).

\paragraph{The base algorithm matters more than the modifications.}
NSGA-III, included only as a many-objective control, reaches hypervolume \num{0.0824} against
\num{0.0762} for the proposed method, a paired difference of $\num{-0.00625}$ at
$p = \num{1.5e-23}$. It does so with a front of about \num{18} solutions against \num{60}, so it
attains higher hypervolume from under a third as many points. With five objectives this is the
regime for which reference-point selection was introduced
\citep{debjain2014evolutionary,zheng2024runtime}, and the result is consistent with that
literature rather than in tension with it.

\paragraph{One of three components does the work.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates each modification. Removing priority-aware initialisation
costs \num{0.0104} hypervolume, so that component is doing real work. The other two are
not: removing adaptive crossover \emph{raises} hypervolume by \num{0.0005} and removing
SLA-aware mutation raises it by \num{0.0006}, both small enough to sit inside run-to-run
noise but neither of them a contribution. Reporting the three as a bundle would have credited
them equally, and that would have been wrong in both directions.

\paragraph{The best configuration is not a significant improvement.}
Placing the one modification that contributes onto the base algorithm that wins gives the
highest hypervolume observed, \num{0.0850}. Against plain NSGA-III the paired difference is
$\num{+0.00254}$ at $p = \num{6.8e-11}$, which does clear $\alpha = \num{0.05}$. It is still
reported as exploratory and is still not claimed, because it was designed after reading the
ablation table on this same data. A post-hoc combination that clears a threshold on the
data that suggested it has not been tested; it has been fitted. Claiming it would be the
same error this paper is about, committed one level up.

\paragraph{A real trade-off, visible in two independent metrics.}
The proposed method loses significantly on workload spread ($+\num{0.22006}$,
$p = \num{1.6e-21}$). Jain's fairness index agrees: \num{0.1207} against
\num{0.1716} for standard NSGA-II. The two metrics were computed independently and could have disagreed. The
method concentrates work on capable groups to gain outcome quality, which is a trade-off an
operator may or may not want. The exponent $p$ in Equation~\ref{eq:powermean} is the parameter
through which that preference is expressed rather than assumed.
